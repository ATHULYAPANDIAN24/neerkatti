WHAT NEERKATTI IS
NEERKATTI shows where Chennai's Pallikaranai wetland became buildings, from free
Sentinel-2 images and Google's Dynamic World V1 AI: 28.1 hectares in 36 patches
since 2018, two-thirds since 2023.


1. UNDERSTANDING & PRIORITISATION
Our mentors told us two things:
  - Make the technical architecture clear.
  - Make our answers clear and simple.
We fixed the architecture first: everything depends on it.


2. HIGH-PRIORITY IMPROVEMENTS
  - A "How it works" diagram in the app, with reasons and limits.
  - Photo check, live: a citizen's photo, read by Gemini in about 3 seconds as
    construction, dumping, sewage, no change or unclear. We store nothing, and
    it still says "needs a ground check".
  - RTI follow-up, live: if a complaint is ignored, the app writes an RTI
    application, and by law the office must answer within 30 days. Then a first
    appeal. The evidence is public and dated.


3. MEASURABLE QUALITY IMPROVEMENT
  - Hand-typed numbers on screen: 1 -> 0.
  - Overclaims in our slides: 2 -> 0.
  - Noise filters: 26 raw detections -> 5 alerts.


4. TESTING & VALIDATION
  - 62 automated tests run on every update. All pass.
  - We tested the live site and the photo check.
  - By eye, 9 of the top 10 patches are real and 1 is unclear; we show it.


5. WEAKNESS & RISK RESOLUTION
  - Risk: Google is retiring our photo check's Gemini model.
    Fixed: now on Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, which is not deprecated.
  - Weakness: the AI misses some marsh, so 28.1 hectares is a minimum.
  - Weakness: 10-metre pixels miss small dumping, so every patch says
    "possible change, needs a ground check".


6. OWNERSHIP, ADAPTABILITY & JUSTIFICATION
  - The AI ran once, through Google Earth Engine's Python API. The website only
    reads the saved results. The one live API call is the optional photo check,
    through our Worker, which holds the key.
  - A proven AI model, not our own: there is no ground truth to train on.
  - Radar is not built yet: it says "Coming", never faked.


WHY IT MATTERS NOW
The Madras High Court is due to hear the marsh's protected-zone case on
11 November 2026. Our nearest alert is about 700 metres from this campus.

Thank you.
