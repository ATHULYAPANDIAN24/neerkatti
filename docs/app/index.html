    The protected marsh and its 1 km zone

    Pallikaranai Marsh is a Ramsar wetland. On 24 Sep 2025 the National Green Tribunal told the authorities to grant no approvals or permissions within a 1 km zone of influence around it (DT Next). In October 2026 a proposed interim buffer was cut by 96%, from 3,358 to 128 ha (New Indian Express, 13 Oct).

    Outline from OpenStreetMap (tagged Reserve Forest and Ramsar site): volunteer-mapped, not the official survey. Our patches were already built over by the 2025 dry season, before the tribunal's order; the final round's alerts would catch new changes inside the zone.

    After the 2021 floods

    A dry-season image shows the marsh at its smallest. On 26 Dec 2021, the clearest image after Chennai's November 2021 floods, the AI labels at least 2,115 ha of the study area water or marsh, against 1,628 ha in the 2018 dry season. 18 of the flagged patches (17.6 ha) were still at least half water or marsh that day. All of them are buildings now.

    How a patch gets flagged
    1. Dynamic World labels every 10 m square of each Sentinel-2 image: water, trees, grass, marsh, crops, shrubs, buildings, bare soil.
    2. For each year we keep the most common label for each square during Feb–May.
    3. A square is flagged if it was water or marsh in 2018 (ignoring the outer ~20 m rim of each water body) and is buildings in both 2025 and 2026.
    4. Patches smaller than about 0.1 ha are dropped as noise.
    5. Each patch gets an AI confidence from Dynamic World's own probabilities: how sure it was of water or marsh in 2018 and of buildings in 2025 and 2026. The weakest of the three sets the level: high from 50%, medium from 35%, low below.
    6. When it was built over: the first dry season from which the AI labels the square buildings every year through 2026. A square that flips back to marsh for one season counts only from after the flip, so the year is a cautious one.
    7. Context: the protected-marsh outline from OpenStreetMap, the AI's labels for 26 Dec 2021 after the floods, and Landsat's water history (JRC, 1984–2021) as a second opinion.

    What's different
    • ISRO's wetland atlas compares snapshots ten years apart (2006–07 vs 2017–18). NEERKATTI looks at every dry season, so it shows when the filling happened and that it is speeding up.
    • TN-SWIP, the state's satellite pilot, sends its alerts to officials (Deccan Chronicle, 23 Aug 2026).
    • NEERKATTI is open to anyone, flags a square only if it stays built-up two years running, checks each patch against the protected marsh and the 2021 floods, and hands over the data. Continuous alerts and complaints come in the final round.
    How far to trust it
    • Dynamic World's own validation gives 86.7% user's accuracy for buildings but only 36.8% producer's accuracy for marsh (Brown et al. 2022, Tables 7–8). It misses marsh, so 28.1 ha is a cautious count.
    • 10 m squares miss small dumping, and monsoon clouds limit us to dry-season images for the timeline.
    • Season labels flicker: some patches read as buildings for one season and marsh the next. The year-by-year bars in each patch show this as it is; the "built over since" year only counts unbroken runs.
    • The top-10 check is a visual check of imagery, not a ground survey.
    • AI confidence is the model's own certainty, not a measure of truth. In the top 10, the unclear patch (#9) is medium, like six of the nine confirmed ones. Low-confidence patches are the ones to check first.
    • The marsh outline is OpenStreetMap's, not the official survey; the 26 Dec 2021 labels come from one image with about 17% of the area under cloud (shown white); Landsat's water history sees open water but not water under marsh plants.

    Why this area and these years

    Why 2018. It is the first dry season with enough images. Sentinel-2B joined Sentinel-2A in 2017, so Dynamic World has 18 images of this area for Feb–May 2018, but only 4 in 2016 and 4 in 2017. A season summary made from 4 images can flip on one cloudy or mislabelled day.

    Why 2026. It is the latest complete dry season, and the rule also needs 2025: a square must be buildings in both.

    Why one watershed. So that every result could be checked: the top 10 by eye, and every patch with the AI's own confidence. Pallikaranai is a Ramsar wetland (site 2481) under pressure now, and one study area at 10 m fits the free Earth Engine tier and a site with no server. The rule itself runs on any area; Chennai-wide runs come next.
